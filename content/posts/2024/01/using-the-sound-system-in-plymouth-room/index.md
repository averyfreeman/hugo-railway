*Plymouth Room*

Plymouth room has a stationary sound system built into the walls (microphone connectors) and ceiling (speakers), with an amplifier lives in middle kitchen in a box above a prep station. Briefly, here's what all of those look like:

![](/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/image-27-1024x504.webp)

*6 speakers in the ceiling: so you know where the sound is coming from*

![](/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/image-28-895x1024.webp)

*1 wall plate per wall, each with 2 connectors, for channels 1 through 4*

XLR input connectors are built into each of the walls around Plymouth room in single-gang, two-connector faceplates. These are labeled 1 - 4, and correspond with the channel of the same name on the amplifier.

![](/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/image-26-768x1024.webp)

*Closer view of a wall plate on a different wall: All four are the same, except different channels.*

Example: Plate shown here has two connectors that correspond with channel 2:

![](/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/PWI3a4s8Bs.webp)

*Each wall plate has a number that corresponds with the same channel on the amp*

![](/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/TPekENdE3B.webp)

*Permanent lair of our protagonist*

Both connectors in each faceplate go to the same channel (aka knob), and are _designed to be microphone inputs **only**_. Line audio (aka music) sources (e.g. iPhone) can be connected to these inputs either by using a **DI converter box**, or by **_turning the volume on the source WAY down_** to avoid distortion and/or feedback.

![](/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/image-29.webp)

*All the things that are relevant to using the amp in Plymouth*

There is also a pair of RCA cables going to the back of the amplifier in the kitchen. If you don't mind (or would rather) the source of music be outside the event room, you can pull the cable out to the right of the amp and plug an RCA cable in there. The RCA connection **does _not_** need a DI box for a line audio source. **_It will not accommodate a microphone_**. This is probably OK, since most guests would prefer talking on the microphone in the event room than the kitchen, but you never know.

### [](#options)Options:

There are **two** ways you can use the Plymouth room sound system:

-   This could be a microphone, phone, computer, etc. but anything other than a mic requires a DI box

-   This input is not suitable for microphones without increasing their output with a preamplifier or a mixer

**Setup style #1:** If you only want music from a source inside the room, use the USB-C (Android) or lightning (iPhone) to stereo 1/8" adapter, then plug the 1/8" to RCA cable and 1/4" adapter into the DI box, and then to a wall connector. *Wall plate connector for channel 3 on the amplifier in the kitchen*

**Setup style #1 w/ a mic, same wall:** You can connect a mic to the XLR connector on the same wall, but its output levels will be controlled with the same knob on the amplifier in the kitchen. This isn't ideal, but if you set the volume on the amplifier to your microphone first, and then set the output level on your music source to a level that works with the microphone level, it could be OK.

Keep in mind, none of the mics can be turned off, so you'll have to either put it somewhere very safe, or run to the kitchen to turn down. Could work OK if done by two coordinated people, one in room with mic and the other in the kitchen. Keep in mind, the speakers will pop if you unplug the mic while the channel is up. _Please don't scare guests_.

**Setup style #1 w/ a mic, different wall:** If you connect the mic to a _different_ wall, it will have a different knob on the amplifier controlling its output level.

**Setup style #1 w/ a mic, line source in kitchen:** You can also use the RCA connectors in the kitchen for any line audio sources, in conjunction with any wall connector for a microphone. Still has the issue of level adjustment being available only in the kitchen, but does not require a DI box.

##### DI box: An important step for sources of music

**The inputs on the walls of the Plymouth room are all microphone-level inputs.** That means they are **high gain**, and **_very sensitive_** - _too sensitive for the outputs of line-level sources._ Therefore, you need to use the DI box in your signal path between your line-level audio source and the inputs on the walls.

Here are the settings I've found work best with the inputs on the Plymouth room amplifier:

![](/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/6QJbvQyDDf-1024x768.webp)

*Proper settings for dampening a line-level source for use with a microphone-level input*

-   **Set attenuation at 20 dB**. _This is the main reason you're using the DI box:_ It drops a -10 dB output to -30dB, making the line audio source match a mic input.

-   **Set mode to 2-channel.** Not particularly relevant to our setup, but it's the most correct.

-   **Set ground to "GND".** This switch **should be flipped to LIFT** if you **hear a humming noise** coming from the channel. It's **_meant to mitigate the hum_**, which is **usually caused by poorly grounded electrical outlets** _(are you plugged into a **power conditioner?**)_

-   **Turn the battery off.** The DI box is **powered by the amplifier** using a feature called **phantom power**, primarily meant for powering _condenser microphones._ It's similar to PoE in networking, look it up if you're curious how it works

## Ready for sound in the Plymouth room?

After you get the configuration you plan to use hooked up, you're ready to turn things on and level set. Here's how I suggest you do that:

-   **Turn the amplifier in the kitchen on.** Turn the **input level** on the channel to **25%**. Turn the master volume to **50%**.

-   **Turn level down on your audio source** (e.g. mixer, computer, phone)

-   **Start making sound** with your source (e.g. speaking into mic, playing music on phone) and if you have a level adjustment, **turn it up**.

-   **Make level adjustments on your source first**, but _if they can't make the audio loud enough,_ **turn levels on the amplifier up.**

##### Troubleshooting: If you don't hear a sound coming from your channel:

-   Check to make sure all the cables in your signal path are connected properly and securely

-   Check the output levels on both your audio **source** and the **amplifier**

-   Check the master volume on the amplifier (knob furthest right - best at 50%)

-   try a different input (if possible, turn the volume down on the amplifier before plugging in a new device, otherwise it'll pop - don't scare guests!)
