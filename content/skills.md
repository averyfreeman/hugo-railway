## The route from signal paths to agent workflows

I built my first desktop PC when I was eight. At The Evergreen State College, I studied liberal arts with a focus on audio engineering and electronic music composition, graduating in 2002. Audio made systems thinking concrete for me: a signal passes through a chain of equipment, each connection has constraints, and the setup only succeeds when another person can use it. I still enjoy that same kind of end-to-end problem solving in software and infrastructure.

My path also ran through business and communication. I worked as an executive language consultant in Tokyo in 2008–2009, explaining financial and economic subjects to multinational executives. I completed an MBA in Management in 2011, with additional accounting, data, and software study around that period. At Berkeley Student Co-op, marketing research and software-adoption discussions brought together user needs, organizational practices, and technology choices.

From 2016 onward, I supported Eastside Big Tom’s systems: point-of-sale and payment equipment, wireless, surveillance, virtualization, storage, remote access, and PCI/DSS controls. One practical improvement extended video retention from about 1.5 months to 7.5 months using iSCSI storage. I also built Bash, Python, and Node.js tools for maintenance, troubleshooting, data parsing, and network-controller APIs.

Event work kept the human side close. As a banquet coordinator and captain, I translated event orders into staffing and setup, coordinated clients and venue teams, and adjusted when requests changed. At Mayflower Park Hotel in 2023–2024, I developed an AV rental offering, wrote procedures, trained employees, and introduced self-selection scheduling. The potential revenue model and the reported 3–8 hours of weekly scheduling time saved describe different things: the first was an estimate; the second was a reported operational change.

Since 2025, licensed process-service work and high-touch club dining have kept careful procedure, trust, and reliable service delivery close to my day-to-day work.

I returned to structured software study through a full-stack program in 2021–2022 and backend, SQL, and DevOps study in 2024. My honors project used Python, Pandas, GeoPandas, and Matplotlib to explore public disclosure data. I now build AI developer tools, agent workflows, MCP servers, plugins, and automation as self-directed practice. These projects show what I am learning and building; they are not a claim of prior production AI employment.
